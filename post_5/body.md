# The Components Driving Bond Yields

We have all heard of bond yields, and how they are the most closely watched indicators of the global economy, but their drivers are often misunderstood. The main three aspects pertaining to fluctuations we see in bond yields are rising Federal reserve policy expectations, inflationary expectations and term premia.

## Federal Reserve Policy Expectations

Bond yields are reflective of Fed policy expectations because their prices and yields are directly influenced by interest rate expectations, which are strongly driven by what investors anticipate the Federal Reserve will do with its policy rates. Firstly interest rates and bond prices have an inverse relationship. When interest rates are expected to increase, bond prices will fall since the new issuance of such bonds will carry an increased yield compared to those currently existing bonds. And therefore those treasuries will be less attractive to investors as they have a lower return. When people expect the Fed to cut rates, bond yields will then decrease and hence the price of existing bonds will increase and vice versa.

## Inflationary Expectations

Ceteris paribus, higher expected inflation will lead investors to demand a higher nominal yield (as to maintain a target level of real returns). How the yield of a given bond responds to rising inflation expectations depends on the horizon over which the increase is expected to occur. For instance, if inflation is expected to increase over the short-run, but abate thereafter, then 2yr nominal yields will increase more than 10yr nominal yields. In essence, the yield fluctuations due to inflationary expectations relies on the expected inflation over that time frame.

## Term Premia

Finally, bond yields are also affected by so-called ‘term premia’. As the name suggests, term premia is the yield premia investors demand to hold longer-term treasuries. Such premia is demanded owing to the elevated degree of uncertainty associated with longer horizons. Essentially as the maturity increases, the term premia does as well, and this is due to the increase in demand for compensation as aspects like inflationary expectations and interest rate fluctuations become more unpredictable.

We can see all factors at play in tariffs. As seen in April, the Fed was expected to cut rates to offset the negative growth of tariffs. Contrary to this however, the Fed could have also been expected to increase rates to preemptively stall the inflationary effects of tariffs. Furthermore at the same time there was more economic uncertainty, causing term premia to rise.
